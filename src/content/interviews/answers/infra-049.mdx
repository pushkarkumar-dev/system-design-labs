## What they're really asking

> **If you only remember one thing, say this in the first minute:**
> "A dedicated endpoint buys isolation and a tail latency I can promise, and it costs utilisation: the customer
> pays for their quiet hours too. A shared pool is cheaper because different customers' bursts mostly don't line up.
> So I'd ask for their load numbers and decide by a rule: dedicate when their own peak fills most of a GPU, when
> their promise is tighter than the pool can hold, or when isolation is in the contract. Otherwise sell them a
> priority tier in the shared pool, and put a price on both."
>
> The rest of this page explains each part of that answer.

The interviewer wants to see whether you can put a number on isolation.

You've made this call before with databases: a customer asks for their own instance instead of a shared cluster.
The same logic applies here, with two differences. The unit of capacity is a GPU replica (one running copy of the
model), which is expensive: an H100 rents for dollars an hour. And the promise customers care about is the **tail
latency**: the **p99**, the time that 99% of requests beat. A shared pool can hold a p99 for everyone only if their
bursts don't all arrive at once.

The prompt comes from one prep article on Together AI, phrased as a customer wanting "a dedicated endpoint with
tight tail-latency guarantees" (<Cite id="s-9597947584" />). A strong answer turns the choice into a rule and a price.

## Clarify first

- **The customer's load.** Steady load, burst size, and how bursty, measured in replicas.
- **The p99 target**, and whether isolation is contractual (compliance, worries about noisy neighbours).
- **Do their bursts line up with everyone else's?** Business hours in one time zone is the usual case.
- **What a replica costs.** "I'll assume one H100 per replica."

## Answer

### Why sharing is cheaper

Every customer's load goes up and down. If you give each customer their own capacity, each needs headroom for its
own peak. In a shared pool, one customer's peak usually lands while others are quiet, so bursts partly cancel. The
pool needs much less total headroom. This is called **statistical multiplexing**, and it is the whole reason shared
pools exist.

```
Dedicated:  [A][A-headroom]  [B][B-headroom]  [C][C-headroom]  ...  20 replicas

Pooled:     [ A + B + C + ... ][ shared headroom ]                  9 replicas
            bursts that don't line up share the same headroom
```

### Follow one set of customers

Take 20 customers. Each uses on average 0.3 of a replica, swings by a typical 0.2 replica (one standard deviation),
and needs headroom of three standard deviations for the latency target. All of these are assumptions.

| Option | Capacity | What to say in the interview |
|---|---|---|
| **Dedicated** | 0.3 + 3 × 0.2 = 0.9, rounded up to **1 replica each, 20 in all** | "Each customer needs a whole replica for a 0.9 peak." |
| **Pooled** | **9 replicas** for all 20 | "Bursts that don't line up share headroom." |
| **One customer's fair share of the pool** | **about 0.43 replica** | "Dedicated costs this customer about 2.3× their pooled share." |

**The assumption that breaks:** this only works if bursts are independent. If all 20 customers peak at 9 a.m. in the
same time zone, or one viral launch hits several of them, the bursts don't cancel and the pool needs more headroom.
Say this before the interviewer does.

### The rule

**Dedicate** when:

- the customer's own peak fills most of a whole replica, so little is wasted;
- their p99 is tighter than the pool can hold when bursts line up; or
- isolation is in the contract.

**Otherwise sell a priority tier** in the shared pool. Their requests go first, lower tiers absorb bursts, and you
measure their p99 against the promise.

### Price it

A dedicated H100 endpoint lists at $5.49 per GPU-hour on Together's page, about **$4,008 a month** always on
([Together pricing](https://www.together.ai/pricing), fetched 2026-10-12). The customer pays for their quiet hours;
say so in the offer.

### What changes on a dedicated endpoint

- **No fair queue across tenants.** There's only one tenant.
- **Autoscaling on their own queue depth and time to first token** (how long until the first word of a reply
  appears).
- **Upgrades start the new copy first** (surge), so their replica never has a gap while a new version loads.
- **An extra replica (N + 1)** if the promise must survive a GPU failure. A single dedicated replica has no failure
  headroom.

Say: "Dedicated isn't a yes-or-no favour. It's a tier with a price, and the price comes from how much of a replica
their own load fills."

## Senior vs Staff

<LevelContrast
  title="Dedicated or shared"
  senior={["Explains utilization loss versus isolation and proposes a priority tier."]}
  staff={["Quantifies pooling, names independence as the assumption that breaks, and turns the decision into pricing tiers rather than exceptions."]}
/>

The difference in plain words: a Senior answer knows sharing is cheaper and offers a middle option. A Staff answer
says how much cheaper, says when that stops being true, and turns the answer into products the sales team can
sell.

## Small-team version

You're usually the customer here. Start on a shared per-token (serverless) endpoint. Buy a dedicated one when your
measured p99 misses your target, or when your steady volume fills a GPU.

At about **$4,008 a month** for one dedicated H100, the threshold is concrete: compare it with your per-token bill
for the same traffic. If your bill is a fraction of that and your p99 is fine, stay shared.

## Pitfalls

- **Deciding by intuition.** Without the customer's load numbers you can't say whether dedicating wastes a lot or a
  little.
- **Assuming bursts are independent.** Customers in one time zone peak together, and the pool's savings shrink.
- **Forgetting that one dedicated replica has no failure headroom.** A promise that survives a GPU failure needs
  N + 1.
