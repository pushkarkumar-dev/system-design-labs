## What they're really asking

> **If you only remember one thing, say this in the first minute:**
> "I wouldn't touch the prompt first. I'd pull the traces for the bad answers and, for each one, find the first
> stage that failed: the answer wasn't in our documents, search didn't find it, search found it but ranked it
> too low, the model had it and ignored it, or the model faithfully repeated an outdated document. Then I'd
> count the buckets and fix the biggest one. Finally I'd add those questions to a labeled set and show the
> number moved on the same questions, before and after."
>
> The rest of this page explains each part of that answer.

The interviewer wants a debugging procedure, not a guess.

A normal backend bug gives you something to hold: an exception, a failing test, the same wrong output for the
same input. A chatbot that answers from your documents gives you none of that. The wrong answer arrives with
status 200, in fluent sentences that sound certain. Nothing in the logs says "failed". And the answer passes
through several stages, any of which could be the cause, while the output looks the same either way. So the
work is to find *which stage* failed, for many bad answers, and count.

### Words you need first

- **RAG** (retrieval-augmented generation): before the language model answers, the system searches your own
  documents and pastes the best passages into the prompt. Finding the passages is **retrieval**; writing the
  answer is **generation**.
- Documents are split into passages called **chunks**. Search returns them in score order, and the best k (the
  **top k**) go into the prompt.
- A **trace** is a log record of one request's path through every stage, like a distributed trace across your
  services.
- An answer is **faithful** when every claim in it is supported by the chunks the model was given. Faithful is not
  the same as correct: the chunk itself can be wrong.

## Clarify first

- **What does "wrong" mean?** "Does it contradict our documents, say something that isn't in them, or repeat
  something outdated?"
- **How many reports, over how much traffic?** *Assume a few dozen flagged answers in two weeks.*
- **Are the retrieved chunk IDs, scores and the final prompt logged?** "If not, logging them is step one."
- **Is there a labeled set** of questions with the right documents marked? *Assume not.*

## Answer

### 1. Reproduce with full traces

For each flagged answer, capture the question, any rewritten version of it, the retrieved chunks with their
ranks and scores, the prompt exactly as sent, the answer, and the model and index versions. Say: "Without the
trace I'm guessing. With it, each bad answer takes a few minutes to classify."

### 2. Classify each failure by its first failing stage

| Stage | Evidence in the trace |
|---|---|
| Coverage gap | No document contains the answer; it should have declined |
| Retrieval miss | The right chunk isn't in the top 50 candidates |
| Ranking miss | It's in the top 50 but not in the top k sent to the model |
| Generation | The supporting text was in the prompt and the answer ignored or contradicted it |
| Stale or wrong source | The answer faithfully follows an outdated or out-of-scope document |

"Confident" is not a stage. The model writes fluent, certain-sounding text whatever its evidence, so tone tells
you nothing.

### Follow one bad answer through it

Here is one case (invented for illustration). A customer asks the support bot: *"Can I get a refund on the annual
plan 45 days after I paid?"* The bot answers: *"Yes, annual plans can be refunded within 60 days."* The real
policy is 30 days.

| Check | What the trace shows | Verdict | What to say in the interview |
|---|---|---|---|
| Is the truth in our documents? | The current policy chunk says 30 days | Not a coverage gap | "The right answer exists." |
| Was it found? | The current policy is at rank 2 of 50 | Not a retrieval miss | "Search found it." |
| Did it reach the model? | Top 3 sent: the 2024 policy, the current policy, an unrelated chunk | Not a ranking miss | "It was in the prompt." |
| What did the answer follow? | It quotes the 2024 policy's "60 days" | Faithful, to the wrong chunk | "A faithfulness check would pass this." |
| Why was the old policy there? | Never removed from the index | **Stale source** | "This is a data bug that looks like a model bug." |

The fix is in the index (remove superseded versions, add dates), not in the prompt or the search engine.

### 3. Count the buckets

Now do the same for every flagged answer. Suppose 40 answers were flagged (counts invented to show the method):
6 coverage gaps, 9 retrieval misses, 8 ranking misses, 12 generation failures, 3 stale sources and 2 ambiguous
questions.

The useful conclusion: a prompt change can only fix the generation bucket, here **at most 12 of 40, or 30%**.
Say: "The triage tells me the ceiling on any prompt fix before I write one."

### 4. Fix by bucket

- **Retrieval.** Check chunks against the **embedder's window**. The embedder is the small model that turns text
  into vectors for search, and it reads only a fixed number of tokens: all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embeds 256 word pieces,
  and longer text is cut silently. Add keyword search (**BM25**, the classic scoring formula behind search
  engines) for product codes and IDs, and rewrite chatty questions into search queries.
- **Ranking.** Add a **reranker** (a slower model that rescores the candidates) or raise k, but only after
  comparing how many right chunks are in the top 50 against the top 5.
- **Generation.** Require citations, permit "I don't know", send fewer distracting chunks, put the strongest
  chunk first, try a stronger model. Check faithfulness before and after.
- **Stale sources.** Re-index, remove old versions, filter by date or scope.
- **Coverage.** Let the bot decline and hand off to a person.

### 5. Prove it

Add the flagged questions to a labeled set. Compute the metric for the failing stage before and after the fix,
on the same questions, and report the difference with an interval. Comparing on the same questions (a **paired
comparison**) cancels most of the noise, because only the questions where the two versions disagree count.

One trap from the rag lab (this site's hands-on retrieval lab) is worth telling. Hybrid search (vector search
plus keyword search) seemed to add nothing, until the bug turned out to be the keyword search's **tokenizer**,
the code that splits text into words. It split on spaces only, so "limit," and "limit" were different words.
Hybrid went from <LabNumber lab="rag" metric="hit@3 on BEIR SciFact" /> (the middle value) to
<LabNumber lab="rag" metric="BM25 tokenizer (SciFact)" /> with a `\w+` tokenizer. Say: "A technique that
doesn't help may be broken. I test each component alone before I give up on it."

own monitoring, not by a customer.

## Small-team version

At a small company this ticket arrives with a screenshot and nothing else. Turn tracing on first, with an
open-source tracer such as Langfuse or a vendor. Then spend a day triaging 40 flagged answers before touching
any code.

- **Use an agent for the first pass.** AI agents are good at the first pass of classification; check 20–30 of
  their labels yourself before trusting the counts.
- **The cost is time, not tools.** 40 traces at an assumed 5 minutes each is about 3.3 hours, and labeling the
  first 50 questions at 3 minutes each another 2.5 hours: roughly **$540** in total at an assumed loaded rate of
  $93 an hour.
- **Tracing.** Self-hosting Langfuse is free apart from the server; buy a hosted tracer if no one wants to run it.
- **Don't buy a new vector database as the fix** until the triage says retrieval is the largest bucket.

## Pitfalls

- **Editing the prompt before looking at what was retrieved.** A prompt change can fix only the generation bucket.
- **Treating "confident" as a signal.** Fluent text sounds certain regardless of the evidence behind it.
- **Trusting faithfulness alone.** An answer that faithfully repeats a stale document is still wrong.
- **Declaring a fix from three re-run examples.** Only a paired metric on a labeled set shows the fix is real.
