This guide is for experienced engineers who want a Senior or Staff role building on top of large language
models, or building the systems that serve them. This page gives you the four roles, how loops changed from
2024 to 2026, the shape of a 2026 loop, and how the research was done. It draws on interview questions that
candidates reported in 2025 and 2026, company pages and news coverage. Every claim links to its source, and
the page says where sources disagree or are thin.

## Who this is for, and who it isn't

**It's for you if** you have roughly 8 to 15 years of backend or distributed-systems work. You know queues,
caches, retries, sharding, consensus and SLOs. You may have called an LLM API, but you haven't shipped a
retrieval system to real users or served a model on GPUs, and you want to know what a 2026 loop will ask.

The good news for you: many AI interview questions are distributed-systems questions with a model in the
middle. One Anthropic Staff candidate described the AI-looking infra questions as "mostly normal infra
questions" once the framing is stripped away (<Cite id="s-51ab9fad72" />). Batching schedulers, permission-aware
indexes, large-file distribution and overload handling all show up in the research. What you don't have yet
is usually narrower: **evals**, **GPU memory math**, and **a clear story about a retrieval or agent system you
built yourself**. The [transition chapter](/system-design-labs/interviews/transition/) is about closing those gaps.

**It's not for you if** you want:

- **ML research or Research Scientist roles.** Pretraining, RL theory and paper-reading rounds are out of scope.
- **Classic ML system design** (ranking, recommendations, fraud). Hello Interview's
  [ML system design guide](https://www.hellointerview.com/learn/ml-system-design) covers that well.
- **Algorithm drills.** You still need them (see below), but plenty of material exists already.

## Four roles, defined

Job titles overlap and companies use them loosely. These definitions are the ones this guide uses.

| Role | What you build | What the loop mostly tests | Covered here |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AI Engineer** | Products on top of models: retrieval (RAG), agents, tool use, evals, guardrails | LLM system design, practical coding with LLM plumbing (retries, streaming, rate limits), evals, a deep dive on your own AI project | Yes: [AI Engineer track](/system-design-labs/interviews/ai-engineer/) |
| **AI Infra Engineer** | The systems that serve and train models: inference serving, batching, GPU scheduling, weight distribution, ML platform | Systems design on inference-shaped problems, concurrent practical coding, parallelism and memory math, failure and overload | Yes: [AI Infra track](/system-design-labs/interviews/ai-infra/) |
| **ML Engineer** | Models themselves: training, fine-tuning, evaluation, data pipelines | ML fundamentals, ML system design, sometimes implementing attention or a transformer block by hand | Only where it overlaps |
| **Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)** | AI systems inside a customer's environment, against the customer's data and constraints | Take-home builds you walk through, live coding (sometimes AI-enabled), customer discovery and scoping | Only where it overlaps |

A few dated facts behind those rows:

- **AI Engineer** as a title was popularized by swyx's June 2023 essay
  ([ai.engineer](https://www.ai.engineer/about)). It tops LinkedIn's 2026 "Jobs on the Rise" list for Australia
  ([HR Leader](https://www.hrleader.com.au/business/27698-ai-engineer-tops-linkedin-s-2026-jobs-on-the-rise-list), Jan 2026);
  the research didn't check other countries' lists.
- **AI Infra** postings read like senior infra postings with a serving stack attached. A Google DeepMind
  Staff posting for inference performance asks for 8 years of experience, work in serving codebases, and
  modelling how batch size and utilization trade latency against cost (<Cite id="s-8e2b61a5c2" />). NVIDIA's
  LLM-inference postings ask for CUDA or Triton, Nsight profiling and contributions to vLLM, SGLang or
  TensorRT-LLM (<Cite id="s-98e1e160c1" />).
- **ML Engineer** loops at big tech still include model-internals questions. Reported 2026 titles include
  implementing the multi-head attention forward pass at Meta (<Cite id="s-be19e40ea6" />) and a transformer
  block and KL divergence at Google (<Cite id="s-4e3fbcaea8" />).
- **FDE** demand jumped in 2025: Indeed data showed applied-AI postings up more than 800% from January to
  September 2025 ([Storyboard18, relaying an FT report](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/openai-anthropic-hire-engineers-who-can-code-and-communicate-with-customers-83622.htm), Nov 2025).
  By May 2026 the Pragmatic Engineer described the work as roughly a quarter coding, half integration and a
  quarter customer-facing ([Pragmatic Engineer](https://blog.pragmaticengineer.com/the-pulse-forward-deployed-engineering-heats-up-again/), 2026-05-24).

### What each track's loop asks about most

Ranked by how often each theme appeared in the first research pass, which is not a measure of the whole
industry.

- **AI Engineer:** retrieval quality, agent reliability, evals, prompt injection and permissions, practical
  LLM-plumbing code, serving latency and cost, AI-assisted coding rounds, a deep dive on your own project,
  customer discovery (for FDE-style roles), and fine-tuning vs RAG.
- **AI Infra (top six of 13):** request batching and inference scheduling, overload and failure, practical
  concurrent code, parallelism and sharding math, GPU fleet scheduling and fairness, and weight distribution
  and cold start.

Each track page ranks these with example questions and Senior vs Staff signals.

## What changed from 2024 to 2026

In 2024, a Senior loop was mostly remote, coding was algorithmic with no AI allowed, and AI-specific design
meant ML system design for ML Engineers. By late 2026, AI tools are allowed in some rounds and banned in
others, often inside the same company. The table below is the guide's summary. Each cell is a report, not a
verified fact; where a company page and a prep site disagree, the company page wins.

### Timeline of AI-in-interview policy changes

- **May 2024.** Fractional AI allows ChatGPT, but not Copilot, in hands-on coding, the earliest dated
  AI-allowed policy found ([Fractional AI](https://www.fractional.ai/blog/technical-interviews-in-the-age-of-llms)).
- **Feb 2025.** Amazon recruiter guidelines say candidates can be disqualified for using AI tools
  (<Cite id="s-102d0e0e4f" />).
- **Jun 2025.** Canva expects candidates to use AI; for backend and frontend roles an AI-Assisted Coding
  round replaces the CS fundamentals screen (<Cite id="s-88d61cb9fa" />). Cursor's CEO says first screens
  allow no AI beyond autocomplete (<Cite id="s-e3eeb47f1f" />).
- **Jul 2025.** Anthropic's candidate guidance: no AI in live interviews or on take-homes unless Anthropic
  says otherwise (<Cite id="s-b4d190c38f" />). 404 Media reports that Meta will let some coding candidates use an
  AI assistant (<Cite id="s-709cf19c59" />).
- **Oct 2025.** Meta's AI-enabled round goes live; a candidate wrote that it "was introduced around October 1"
  (<Cite id="s-2421538424" />). interviewing.io reports that CoderPad now has cheating detection on by
  default (<Cite id="s-33ab0ba309" />).
- **Jan 2026.** Anthropic explains how models beat its performance take-home, where AI is explicitly allowed
  (<Cite id="s-a8c29f7dce" />).
- **May 2026.** Google confirms a pilot: from H2 2026, junior and mid-level candidates on select US teams may
  use Gemini in a "code comprehension" round (<Cite id="s-90bac7273b" />, <Cite id="s-b88d89c103" />). As of
  2026-10-11, no first-hand report shows it running.
- **Aug 2026.** A Stripe Staff candidate reports an AI agent-coding round and, in their own read, was marked down for
  letting the agent drive (<Cite id="s-8e0784406e" />). A LinkedIn candidate reports an AI coding round with a built-in
  assistant (<Cite id="s-8e19cb9489" />).
- **Sep–Oct 2026.** Two Amazon online assessments include an "AI debug" task on a small existing codebase
  (<Cite id="s-50480ec72b" />, <Cite id="s-26efabd25c" />). Perplexity's interview portal lists an Agentic
  Coding round "requiring the use of frontier coding agents" (<Cite id="s-2b79c76453" />, page undated,
  checked 2026-10-11).

## Anatomy of a 2026 Senior or Staff loop

No two companies run the same loop. This is the common shape across the reports, in the usual order.

1. **Recruiter screen.** Ask two questions here: which rounds allow AI tools, and what level the role is
   opened at. Rules vary inside one company: an Apple round advertised as "any tool allowed" used no tool
   at all (<Cite id="s-fdb7a967ba" />).
2. **Online assessment or technical screen.** Anthropic's is reported as a timed CodeSignal test with
   progressive levels and many edge-case tests (<Cite id="s-6a7cff749e" />). Two 2026 Amazon reports describe an "AI
   debug" task on a small existing codebase (<Cite id="s-50480ec72b" />, <Cite id="s-26efabd25c" />).
3. **Coding: practical vs DSA.** Big tech keeps at least one algorithm round, with fewer copied problems and
   more multi-part ones (<Cite id="s-e21c7d5689" />). AI labs lean practical: a small database, a credit-tracking
   service or a resumable iterator, each with several behaviors to implement (<Cite id="s-4d55d9b987" />). Infra loops add concurrency: crawlers, thread-safe caches, file processing.
4. **AI-assisted coding.** Meta's version, as Hello Interview describes it: 60 minutes in a multi-file
   CoderPad project, in three phases (fix a bug, build the core feature, optimize), with an assistant that
   answers in chat but can't edit files (<Cite id="s-810bf5f1ff" />). Shopify's starts from an empty repo in
   your own IDE with any tool (<Cite id="s-5e3f4c7957" />).
5. **LLM system design.** For AI Engineer roles: an enterprise RAG assistant with permissions, a support agent
   with tools and human handoff. For AI Infra: a batched inference API, distributing large files to many
   machines, a GPU scheduler. One Anthropic Senior candidate's design round opened as "implement an API" for
   sampling from LLMs with batching (<Cite id="s-42f1c7f6dd" />).
6. **Project presentation or deep dive.** OpenAI's onsite reportedly includes a 45-minute presentation with
   slides (<Cite id="s-74b419b5c0" />). An Anthropic infra guide describes a 15–25 minute project
   presentation (<Cite id="s-e23c30a3c6" />). Expect "what did *you* do" and "how did you measure it".
7. **Values and behavioral.** At Anthropic, a recruiter warned one candidate that the values round is where
   people usually fail (<Cite id="s-42f1c7f6dd" />). Amazon assesses every answer against its Leadership
   Principles with a Bar Raiser from outside the team (<Cite id="s-2734934856" />).
8. **References and team match.** References can come early or late. One Databricks candidate passed the
   onsite and was rejected after the reference stage (<Cite id="s-72f8a26584" />).

<AINative title="AI-assisted rounds grade verification, not prompting">
Hello Interview reports that Meta grades its AI-enabled round on the same four things as other coding rounds:
problem solving, code quality, verification and communication. It is not scoring prompt engineering
(<Cite id="s-810bf5f1ff" />). Canva says "the most successful candidates didn't just prompt AI and accept whatever it
generated" (<Cite id="s-88d61cb9fa" />). A LinkedIn candidate finished 2 of 4 tasks and says most of the time
went to fixing the AI's mistakes (<Cite id="s-8e19cb9489" />). Practise owning the plan, running tests often and
explaining every line the assistant wrote. The [AI-native chapter](/system-design-labs/interviews/ai-native/)
has drills.
</AINative>

### What separates Senior from Staff

Across the reports, coding mostly filters and the design and behavioral rounds set the level. In a Blind
thread about a Meta E6 "AI native" role, commenters describe coding as effectively pass or fail, with design
and behavioral setting the level (<Cite id="s-2a15ccde37" />). Hello Interview's E6 guide calls design "the part
of the loop that most drives your E6 level determination" (<Cite id="s-be529bb3fa" />). At Staff, the round mix itself is disputed:
Hello Interview says Meta's AI round runs up to E7/M2 alongside a classic round, while interviewing.io says
E7+/M1 get a single coding round and it is AI-assisted (<Cite id="s-810bf5f1ff" />, <Cite id="s-f5c1072815" />).
Neither is Meta documentation.

Staff signals in the reports are mostly about judgment. Anthropic used design-doc reviews and critiques where
the emphasis was judgment and communication rather than calculation (<Cite id="s-4e3dc29a95" />,
<Cite id="s-eab681003c" />). One Staff inference candidate believes pushing back on the interviewer's premise
is what sank them (<Cite id="s-4e04a1689c" />). The Stripe Staff rejection's feedback was to design from the
actual requirements, not a template (<Cite id="s-8e0784406e" />).

## How to use this guide

Pick the track that matches the jobs you're applying for, then work outward.

- [AI Engineer](/system-design-labs/interviews/ai-engineer/) and [AI Infra](/system-design-labs/interviews/ai-infra/)
  tracks: ranked themes, Senior vs Staff signals, rejection reasons and a lab-backed prep path. The infra page
  adds a back-of-envelope sheet.
- [Walkthroughs](/system-design-labs/interviews/walkthroughs/): worked answers to reported questions, added
  as each one is finished.
- [Question bank](/system-design-labs/interviews/questions/): every reported question found, each with its
  source, type, confidence and date, filterable by track, theme, round and company.
- [Companies](/system-design-labs/interviews/companies/): reported loops and AI-tool policies, each with a
  last-verified date.
- [The AI-native engineer](/system-design-labs/interviews/ai-native/): the evidence on engineers directing
  agents, and what AI-assisted rounds grade.
- [Making the transition](/system-design-labs/interviews/transition/): what carries over, the gaps, a 12-week
  plan and leveling.

The guide points to the site's labs as "build it to prove it" material. Each lab link shows its status. A
**proof** lab has real code and tests. A **partial** lab has mocked parts, bookkeeping-only parts or failing
tests, and the link says which. Lab numbers appear only with a label that says whether they were measured, calculated or estimated.

## How this was researched, and its limits

The research was done on **2026-10-11** by AI research agents that searched the web and fetched pages, in two
passes; the second targeted companies and forums the first missed. Every question, company claim and
statistic on these pages traces to a URL in the saved reports.

Each source carries a type: **official** (the company's own page), **first-hand** (a candidate's account),
**aggregator** (candidate-submitted question titles; only the title counts), **secondary** (a prep site or
newsletter), **news**, or **snippet** (the page was blocked and only a search summary was seen). Confidence
follows: a single anonymous post is low, snippet-only is always low, and aggregator- or prep-site-only is at
most medium. A typical row looks like <SourceTag type="first-hand" confidence="medium" />.

What the research could not do:

- **Reddit was unreachable.** No Reddit thread was read. Mirrors that block automated access were respected.
- **Glassdoor, LeetCode Discuss, Medium and OpenAI's interview guide** mostly returned errors, so those claims
  rest on search snippets and are labelled as such.
- **The web-search quota ran out** partway through the first pass, so some planned searches never ran.
- **The question banks skew toward OpenAI and Anthropic.** Aggregators list far more questions for them. In the
  data as of 2026-10-11, OpenAI is the most-cited company in the AI Engineer bank and Anthropic in the AI
  Infra bank, each tagged on roughly one question in five. First-hand AI-infra questions from big tech are
  still rare.
- **Some dates are approximate.** Blind hides the year on current-year posts; Aced shows relative dates.
- **Some things were looked for and not found:** an official Senior vs Staff rubric for AI roles at any
  company, a standalone eval-design round at a named company, and systematic evidence that engineers moving
  into AI roles are down-leveled.
- **One third-party policy tracker was not used:** it cites nothing and contradicts Anthropic's own page.

Policies here change every few months. Each company card shows when it was last checked. If you find a
newer or better source, trust it over this page.
