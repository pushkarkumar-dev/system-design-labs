If you've spent years on backend or distributed systems, you already know most of what an AI Infra loop
tests, and a good part of an AI Engineer loop. Many reported questions are systems questions with a model in
the middle. What you're missing is narrower than it looks, and this chapter's plan closes it in about three
months of part-time work.

This chapter covers what carries over, which doors are realistic, the gaps that actually get people rejected,
a 12-week plan built on the site's labs, what to put in a portfolio, and what is known (very little) about
leveling.

## What you already know, in AI terms

Most of the concepts transfer. What changes is the unit you count (tokens, not requests), the resource you
run out of (GPU memory, not CPU), and the meaning of "correct" (a score on an eval set, not a status code).

| You already know | The AI version | What's actually new | Build it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Caching, cache keys, CDNs | Prefix and KV-cache reuse: requests that share a prompt prefix reuse its stored keys and values instead of recomputing them | The cache lives in GPU memory, is sized per token, and hits only on an exact token prefix | <LabRef lab="llm-inference" inline />, <LabRef lab="cdn-edge" inline /> |
| Load balancing, consistent hashing | Token- and KV-aware routing: route on queued tokens and free KV memory; keep a conversation on the replica holding its prefix | Request count is a poor load signal when request sizes differ by orders of magnitude | <LabRef lab="load-balancer" inline />, <LabRef lab="consistent-hashing" inline /> |
| Queues, backpressure | Continuous batching and admission control: new requests join the running batch between decode steps, and you admit one only if its KV cache fits | The batch changes every step, and memory is what runs out | <LabRef lab="message-queue" inline />, <LabRef lab="llm-inference" inline /> |
| Latency SLOs (p50, p99) | Time to first token (TTFT) and time per output token (TPOT) as separate budgets | Prefill is compute-bound and decode is memory-bandwidth-bound, so they are tuned differently | <LabRef lab="observability" inline /> |
| Sharding, partitioning | Tensor, pipeline and expert parallelism | The network sits inside one forward pass; collectives such as all-reduce are on the critical path | No lab yet |
| Canary deploys, feature flags | Model and prompt rollouts behind eval gates, with shadow traffic | "Better" is a score with a confidence interval | <LabRef lab="feature-flags" inline />, <LabRef lab="cicd-engine" inline />, <LabRef lab="llm-eval" inline /> |
| Idempotent retries, circuit breakers | Failover across model providers and regions | Retries cost tokens, and an agent's tool call can have side effects, so it isn't automatically idempotent | <LabRef lab="load-balancer" inline /> |
| Rate limiting per request | Token- and credit-based limits per tenant | Output length, and so cost, is known only when generation ends: reserve, then settle | <LabRef lab="rate-limiter" inline /> (counts requests only) |
| Cold starts, image pulls | Loading and distributing model weights of tens to hundreds of GB | Bandwidth math dominates; peer-to-peer and cache tiers matter | <LabRef lab="faas-runtime" inline />, <LabRef lab="cdn-edge" inline /> |
| Unit and integration tests | Evals: graded task sets, run many times, read statistically | Outputs are nondeterministic, so a single pass or fail means little | <LabRef lab="llm-eval" inline /> |
| Search, inverted indexes | Hybrid retrieval: keyword (BM25) plus dense vectors plus a reranker | Quality is recall@k and faithfulness, not just latency | <LabRef lab="rag" inline />, <LabRef lab="vector-index" inline /> |
| AuthZ, multi-tenancy | Permission-aware retrieval, tool permissions, prompt-injection defence | Untrusted text in the context can act like an instruction | No lab yet |
| Tracing and metrics | LLM observability: tokens and cost per request, TTFT, prompt and response capture | Quality signals sit next to latency and errors | <LabRef lab="observability" inline /> (generic tracing) |

Prep guides make the serving points explicitly: treat TTFT and inter-token latency as separate budgets and
autoscale on queue depth or TTFT, not GPU utilization (<Cite id="s-f440daaea4" />), and say out loud that prefill
is compute-bound and decode memory-bound
([techinterview.org serving guide](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233476388/designing-an-llm-serving-system/)).
Reported infra prompts follow the same pattern: distributing large files to many machines with limited
bandwidth (<Cite id="s-e23c30a3c6" />), a warm GPU-container pool and fast 40 GB model cold starts
(<Cite id="s-86890a1069" />), and a resilient LLM provider pool (<Cite id="s-a90c8401d4" />).

## The realistic doors

Research-scientist roles are not a realistic switch without research output. Three other doors are.

### Inference and ML platform infrastructure

This is the closest fit for most distributed-systems engineers.

- Anthropic's careers page says "About half our technical staff had no prior ML experience"
  (<Cite id="s-131d7342e9" />). Its performance-engineering post says "People with good fundamentals can learn
  specifics on the job" (<Cite id="s-a8c29f7dce" />, 2026-01-21).
- In a 2024 Blind thread, a Google commenter said ML infra is "a lot easier to break into with just a
  bachelor's", and a Tesla commenter added "Basically distributed systems programming"
  ([Blind](https://www.teamblind.com/post/ml-engineer-without-phd-vut13vkw), Oct 2024).
  Ilya Reznik says that for infra roles, CS fundamentals matter more than math
  ([Taro](https://www.jointaro.com/question/YqrpDDCPbsGpg0lwrKXY/looking-to-transition-to-ml-engineer-what-do-you-want-to-know/), ~Dec 2024).
- A Staff candidate for a Databricks AI-platform team reports being asked about batched write-ahead-log writes
  three times and a key-value store with locking, and no LLM questions at all (<Cite id="s-d6af500d84" />).

### Applied AI engineering

If you've built product backends, APIs, search or data pipelines, this door is open too. Janvi Kalra
interviewed at 46 companies and became an AI Engineer at OpenAI. Her loops mixed LeetCode, system design,
build-an-app take-homes and LLM-concept deep dives. The advice: prepare as for a SWE loop and build LLM products
on the side. Her own way in was an internal LLM project at Coda
([Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/from-software-engineer-to-ai-engineer), May 2025).

### Forward deployed engineering

If you like customers and ambiguity, FDE roles are growing fast but drifting toward consulting: roughly a
quarter coding, half integration and a quarter customer-facing by May 2026
([Pragmatic Engineer](https://blog.pragmaticengineer.com/the-pulse-forward-deployed-engineering-heats-up-again/)).
One first-hand OpenAI FDE loop had a one-week take-home (semantic search that ChatGPT can call), a walkthrough,
then an AI-enabled live coding screen (<Cite id="s-05342b6eb9" />).

### Internal moves and referrals beat cold applications

- Replies to a Block manager on Blind said it's easier to move into ML infra first, ideally internally
  ([Blind](https://www.teamblind.com/post/transition-from-backend-engineering-to-ai-n6stczoc), Mar 2025).
  A Hacker News thread says the same, with one engineer reporting a year of study before switching
  ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40797858), Jun 2024).
- Anonymous Blind commenters say most frontier-lab hires come through former managers
  ([Blind](https://www.teamblind.com/post/how-does-one-actually-get-hired-at-anthropicopenai-1l315yhr)). A
  recruiter told the Pragmatic Engineer that inbound candidates fail loops more often than sourced ones
  ([Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/state-of-the-tech-market-in-2025-hiring-managers), Oct 2025).

**Degrees are disputed.** Reznik calls the switch "an uphill battle" without a graduate degree. Against that
are Anthropic's own figure above and a secondary tracker that found 73% of 59 ML-infra postings state no
degree requirement ([profrod.ai](https://www.profrod.ai/roles/ml-infrastructure-engineer)).

## The gaps that actually matter

### 1. Evals

This is the gap that separates applied candidates most clearly. Eugene Yan and Jason Liu, writing as hiring
managers, say even engineers who only call APIs should build eval harnesses, track quality over time and add
guardrails ([Eugene Yan](https://eugeneyan.com/writing/how-to-interview/), Jul 2024). A rejected Harvey
candidate's coding round ended with how they would improve the eval's recall and hit rate
(<Cite id="s-74265be7ec" />). One prep guide warns that if you can't define good retrieval as a number,
the interviewer "assumes you tuned yours by feel" (<Cite id="s-7d03a572e0" />).

### 2. Nondeterminism

Your instincts about tests assume the same input gives the same output. With models it doesn't: sampling,
provider updates and long contexts all change answers. An eval result is a sample, so you need enough tasks,
repeated runs and a confidence interval before you call a change better. The <LabRef lab="llm-eval" inline />
lab builds bootstrap confidence intervals. The [AI-native chapter](/system-design-labs/interviews/ai-native/)
covers pass@k vs pass^k.

### 3. GPU memory math

Infra loops expect you to work out what fits on a GPU from first principles, out loud. Learn the formulas, not
the multipliers: prep sources quote continuous-batching gains of about 2× (<Cite id="s-f440daaea4" />),
3–5× ([serving guide](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233476388/designing-an-llm-serving-system/)) and 10–20×
([LLM API guide](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233460079/design-an-llm-inference-api/)).

### 4. A project story that survives a deep dive

A Meta SWE screen asked a candidate to explain an AI system they had designed, and they felt they undersold it
(<Cite id="s-a7b843c47f" />). Harvey's onsite included a project deep dive (<Cite id="s-74265be7ec" />). An
Anthropic FDE hiring manager reportedly asked why the candidate used an LLM at all and how they knew it fit
(<Cite id="s-cb39678bbd" />). A LinkedIn Senior candidate's
project discussion on an LLM integration framework went into token handling and per-query vs per-token
pricing (<Cite id="s-1980a339c0" />). The OpenAI FDE take-home review probed how specific the retrieval should
be (<Cite id="s-05342b6eb9" />). Have answers ready for: the baseline, the eval, the numbers, the failure you
found, the cost per request, and what you'd change.

### 5. Enough model vocabulary

You don't need research math, but you do need the basics cold. Databricks AI Engineer recruiter and phone
screens asked "Describe RAG" and "What is a transformer?"
([Aced](https://www.aced.io/experiences/databricks-ai-engineer-interview-974d8c), 2026). An Apple GenAI round
reportedly opened with what the KV cache is and how it helps inference (<Cite id="s-1b8091345c" />, snippet).

<Pitfall title="API calls are not an AI background">
In a thread from a GenAI-experienced engineer who couldn't get interviews, a commenter at Google said
"prompt engineering on top of GPT" isn't enough
([Blind](https://www.teamblind.com/post/have-genai-llm-experience-cant-get-interviews-advice-bkf2bdgb), Dec 2024).
A wrapper around a chat API shows you can call an API. It doesn't show retrieval quality, evals or serving.
</Pitfall>

## A 12-week study plan

The plan assumes part-time study alongside a job. If you have 10 weeks, combine weeks 1 and 2, and skip
week 9 if you're applying only to AI Engineer roles. Partial labs carry a pill; hover it to see what is mocked or which tests fail.
Finishing the missing part of a partial lab yourself, such as adding token streaming and TTFT/TPOT timing to
llm-inference, is a portfolio item in its own right.

| Weeks | Focus | Labs | By the end you can |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | How models work | <LabRef lab="tokenizer" inline />, <LabRef lab="transformer" inline /> | Explain tokens and their cost, attention and the KV cache; compute KV bytes per token for a GQA model |
| 3–4 | Inference | <LabRef lab="llm-inference" inline /> (partial), <LabRef lab="speculative-decoding" inline /> (partial), <LabRef lab="model-quantizer" inline /> (partial) | Explain prefill vs decode and batching; derive speculative-decoding gains; explain why 4-bit weights need per-group scales |
| 5–6 | Retrieval | <LabRef lab="embedding-pipeline" inline />, <LabRef lab="vector-index" inline />, <LabRef lab="rag" inline /> | Measure recall@k on your own documents; defend hybrid search and reranking choices |
| 7 | Evals | <LabRef lab="llm-eval" inline /> | Build an eval set with confidence intervals and a CI gate |
| 8 | Agents and tools | <LabRef lab="agent-framework" inline />, <LabRef lab="mcp-server" inline /> | Swap the mock model for a real one; add budgets and permission checks |
| 9 | Infra patterns, made token-aware | <LabRef lab="rate-limiter" inline />, <LabRef lab="load-balancer" inline />, <LabRef lab="observability" inline /> | Turn request-count limits into token budgets; record TTFT and TPOT |
| 10 | Design practice | [Walkthroughs](/system-design-labs/interviews/walkthroughs/), [question bank](/system-design-labs/interviews/questions/) | Two timed mock designs for your track |
| 11 | Coding practice | The AI-assisted drill in the [AI-native chapter](/system-design-labs/interviews/ai-native/) | One AI-assisted session and two no-AI sessions |
| 12 | Story and loop prep | Your own project | A 30-minute project presentation, behavioral stories, questions for the recruiter |

## Portfolio: what counts

**Work that other people have validated beats courses.**

- A Staff engineer with 16 years of experience asked on Blind whether an interview-prep course was enough.
  Replies said no: build projects and keep taking AI interviews until you learn what gets asked
  ([Blind](https://www.teamblind.com/post/switching-to-aiml-from-backend-engineer-role-dcoaypjg), Apr 2025).
- A Microsoft engineer with an ML master's said a small agents project on their résumé brought no recruiter
  interest ([Blind](https://www.teamblind.com/post/software-engineer-to-ai-engineer-career-transition-path-2z3ifcwa), Jun 2025).
- Reznik says the only accomplishments he cares about as a hiring manager are "validated by other people":
  a project 10,000 people use, an open-source contribution a maintainer accepts, a freelance project
  ([Taro](https://www.jointaro.com/question/YqrpDDCPbsGpg0lwrKXY/looking-to-transition-to-ml-engineer-what-do-you-want-to-know/)).
- Job postings say the same. NVIDIA names contributions to vLLM, SGLang or TensorRT-LLM
  (<Cite id="s-98e1e160c1" />), and a DeepMind Staff inference posting prefers contributions to serving
  frameworks (<Cite id="s-8e2b61a5c2" />). Anthropic asks you to put independent research, blog posts or
  open-source work at the top of your résumé (<Cite id="s-131d7342e9" />), and has published its original
  performance take-home as an open challenge (<Cite id="s-a8c29f7dce" />, <Cite id="s-90e7a87996" />).

What a strong project looks like:

- **One project, taken deep,** rather than five demos.
- **Real users or a public benchmark,** so someone other than you has checked it.
- **Measured numbers with the hardware stated,** and an eval you can show.
- **A write-up of what failed** and what you changed because of it.
- **The cost per request,** in tokens and money.

The site's labs are a starting point, not a portfolio. On their own they are code only you have run. Extend
one: replace the mock model with a real one, measure on your hardware, publish the write-up, or send the fix
upstream to a real project.

<Pitfall title="Match the project to the team">
An Uber Senior MLE candidate presented a GenAI agent project to a pricing and marketplace team. The interviewer
said it "might not be a great match", and HR raised a possible down-level from L5A to L4; the candidate
withdrew ([PracHub](https://prachub.com/interview-experiences/uber-seniorplus-machine-learning-engineer-interview-experience-withdrew-after-a-possible-l5a-to-l4-downlevel), Apr 2026).
Lead with the project closest to the team's actual work.
</Pitfall>

## Leveling and down-leveling

**Everything in this section is anecdote.** The research found no first-hand account of an experienced backend
engineer being down-leveled *because* they moved into an AI role, and no systematic data either way.

- **Down-leveling happens for ordinary reasons,** in AI teams too. Blind previews describe a Meta hiring
  committee down-leveling a candidate to E4 after an onsite that included an AI coding round, and a Google
  candidate who passed L6 and team match and was then set to L5 by headcount review
  ([Blind search](https://www.teamblind.com/search/downleveled), years not shown).
- **Frontier labs map levels inconsistently.** A "high performing E6 at Meta" got Staff offers from OpenAI and
  Anthropic, at L5 from one and L6 from the other; commenters said "OpenAI 5 == staff"
  ([Blind](https://www.teamblind.com/post/how-does-leveling-at-openai-and-anthropic-work-dlmzzycd)).
  interviewing.io says OpenAI "has a reputation for downleveling candidates coming in", without a source
  (<Cite id="s-74b419b5c0" />).
- **Skepticism of Staff+ converts** comes from Reznik, who is "very apprehensive of people transitioning as
  staff+" because "you are not worth the salary at that level for a long time"
  ([Taro](https://www.jointaro.com/question/YqrpDDCPbsGpg0lwrKXY/looking-to-transition-to-ml-engineer-what-do-you-want-to-know/)).
- **Level calibration can sink a good loop.** A Microsoft Senior+ data-science candidate with positive rounds was
  rejected as reading "more like a DS II than a Senior" (<Cite id="s-d4dbe6f90b" />).
- **Counter-evidence exists.** A July 2024 Compa analysis of offers since 2022 found AI engineering offers skew *more* senior: 21.5% were
  below P3, against 36.5% of software engineering offers, with an undisclosed sample size
  ([Compa](https://compapeergroup.substack.com/p/ai-eng-offers-skew-more-senior-levels)). An Apple engineer got
  two AI-infra offers at the same level with significant raises
  ([Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/tech-jobs-market-in-2026-part-3-hiring), 2026-07-07).
  Levels.fyi measured an 18.7% pay premium for AI-focused Staff engineers in 2025
  ([Levels.fyi](https://www.levels.fyi/blog/ai-engineer-compensation-trends-q3-2025.html)).

What to do with this: ask the recruiter which level the role is opened at before the onsite, ask whether the
design and behavioral rounds decide the level (at Meta they reportedly do: <Cite id="s-be529bb3fa" />), and prepare your design rounds at
the level you want, not the level you fear.

## Common rejection reasons

From candidate reports, most-cited first. Each is the candidate's account or a prep site's summary, not stated
company feedback, unless it says otherwise.

1. **Unfinished practical coding or follow-ups.** Three Anthropic infra reports point to an unfinished
   follow-up or task (<Cite id="s-2348518c66" />, <Cite id="s-4e04a1689c" />, <Cite id="s-e9fee3985a" />).
   Senior candidates at Sierra and Harvey were rejected after multi-part practical coding rounds; the Harvey
   candidate's advice is to be "really fast" (<Cite id="s-aa5a42ddc5" />, <Cite id="s-956f0bbc95" />).
2. **The values or culture round at Anthropic.** A recruiter warned one candidate it is where people usually
   fail (<Cite id="s-42f1c7f6dd" />). A Senior Staff candidate thinks it sank his loop, and a commenter on his
   thread says it sank theirs (<Cite id="s-e98cbef9cd" />).
3. **No validated AI work** to talk about (the Microsoft engineer's Blind thread above).
4. **Overcomplicated or template designs.** The Harvey candidate thinks they overcomplicated a multi-agent
   design (<Cite id="s-74265be7ec" />). Stripe's Staff feedback was to design from the actual requirements
   (<Cite id="s-8e0784406e" />).
5. **Fighting the design premise** instead of accepting it and adding depth (<Cite id="s-4e04a1689c" />).
6. **Miscalibrated AI use** in either direction (<Cite id="s-8e0784406e" />, <Cite id="s-443984bc07" />).
7. **Hand-waved evaluation**, such as an LLM judge never checked against human labels; this one comes from a
   prep guide's list of weak answers, not a rejection report (<Cite id="s-7d03a572e0" />).
8. **Losing it at the reference stage** after passing the onsite (<Cite id="s-72f8a26584" />).
9. **Applying cold** instead of being referred or sourced (the Pragmatic Engineer recruiter quote above).
