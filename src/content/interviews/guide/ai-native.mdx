A claim runs through hiring in 2025 and 2026: **one engineer increasingly owns the whole software and ML
lifecycle by directing AI agents.** Design, code, tests, deploys, evals and monitoring, done by one person
who decides, delegates and checks. If it's true, interviews should test how well you direct and verify
agents. If it's false, they should keep testing whether you can do the work yourself.

This chapter weighs the evidence for and against, gives a verdict, and turns it into skills and practice
drills. Most numbers below come from studies with clear limits. Each is dated and linked, and the limits are
stated next to the number.

## The claim, in two strengths

- **Strong form:** a few engineers with agents can replace whole teams across most kinds of software.
- **Narrow form:** teams get fewer, more senior engineers per product surface. Each covers more of the
  lifecycle by directing agents, and the work that limits them is **checking the agents' output**.

The evidence below supports the narrow form much better than the strong form. Keep the two apart when you
talk about this in an interview. Claiming the strong form is an easy way to sound like you haven't looked at
the data.

## Evidence for

### Employers now expect it

Several large employers have tied headcount or performance reviews to AI use. They differ in how far they go.

- **Shopify** (memo posted 2025-04-07): "Reflexive AI usage is now a baseline expectation." Teams must show
  why AI can't do a job before asking for more headcount
  ([Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2025/04/08/internal-memo-shopify-ceo-declares-ai-non-optional/)).
- **Duolingo** (Apr 2025): it will gradually stop using contractors for work AI can handle, and AI use
  becomes part of performance evaluation ([Pure AI](https://pureai.com/Articles/2025/04/29/Duolingo-Leans-Into-AI.aspx)).
- **Microsoft** (memo reported Jul 2025): Developer Division president Julia Liuson wrote that "Using AI is no
  longer optional",
  with AI use feeding holistic performance reflections; a formal metric was only being considered
  ([UNLEASH](https://www.unleash.ai/artificial-intelligence/ai-is-no-longer-optional-at-microsoft-is-this-the-right-way-to-drive-workplace-ai-adoption/)).
- **Coinbase** (Aug 2025): every engineer had to set up Cursor and Copilot within about a week, and the CEO
  said about a third of new code was AI-written, with a 50% target
  ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/coinbase-ceo-fired-software-engineers-who-didnt-adopt-ai/496250)).
- **Meta** (Nov 2025): "AI-driven impact" becomes a core expectation in 2026 reviews, judged on personal
  productivity, tools that help the team, and contribution to Meta's AI work
  ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/meta-may-lower-appraisals-for-employees-who-dont-use-ai-enough-84313.htm)).
- **Google** (Apr 2026): Sundar Pichai said about 75% of new code is AI-generated, up from 25% in 2024.
  Semafor called such claims "pretty impossible to fact-check"
  ([Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/04/24/2026/google-ceo-says-75-of-companys-new-code-is-ai-generated)).

Note the pattern. Shopify, Meta and Microsoft judge AI-driven *impact*, not raw usage counts. That matters
for how you describe your own AI use in a behavioral round (see the end of this chapter).

### Some measurements show real gains

- **Anthropic's internal study** (2025-12-02), 132 engineers and 200,000 Claude Code transcripts: Claude was
  used in 59% of work, up from 28%; merged pull requests per engineer per day rose 67%; and 27% of
  AI-assisted work would not otherwise have been done. Engineers described working across front-end,
  database and API code they used to avoid. The authors flag selection and social-desirability bias
  ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/how-ai-is-transforming-work-at-anthropic)).
- **DORA 2025** (2025-09-23): unlike 2024, AI adoption now has a positive relationship with delivery
  throughput and product performance; 90% of respondents use AI
  ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-the-2025-dora-report)).
- **Controlled trials on scoped tasks:** a Google trial with 96 engineers found about 21% less time on an
  enterprise task, with a wide confidence interval ([arXiv, Oct 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12944)).
  A GitHub Copilot trial found a single well-specified JavaScript task done 55.8% faster
  ([arXiv, Feb 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06590)).

### Small teams, big output

- **Lovable** passed $100M in annual recurring revenue with 45 full-time employees
  ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/23/eight-months-in-swedish-unicorn-lovable-crosses-the-100m-arr-milestone/), 2025-07-23).
- **Base44**, solo-founded and bootstrapped, was bought by Wix for about $80M plus earn-outs
  ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-1001513267), Jun 2025).
- **Demand is shifting toward senior and AI-titled roles.** Indeed's Hiring Lab found that senior roles made
  up 71% of the net increase in US software postings from May 2025 to May 2026, and titles mentioning AI 37%.
  The author calls this correlation, not causation, and preliminary
  ([Indeed Hiring Lab](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/2026/07/08/ai-and-job-postings-from-destruction-to-creation/), 2026-07-08).
  Salesforce says it tripled its Forward Deployed Engineer team in six months
  ([Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/forward-deployed-engineers-making-ai-jobs-more-human/), Mar 2026).

## Evidence against

### The best-controlled studies are weak or negative

- **METR, early 2025:** 16 experienced open-source developers worked 246 real tasks. With
  AI allowed, tasks took **19% longer** (confidence interval +2% to +39%). The developers expected a 24%
  speedup and afterwards still believed they had been about 20% faster
  ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/), 2025-07-10).
- **METR follow-up:** 57 developers and 800+ tasks with late-2025 tools. Returning developers were −18% time
  (CI −38% to +9%). METR says developers are likely faster now, but the data is "only very weak evidence"
  for the size of the gain, partly because many refused to work without AI and timing breaks down when people
  run several agents at once ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/), 2026-02-24).
- **DORA:** in 2024, a 25% rise in AI adoption was associated with about 7.2% lower delivery stability
  ([InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2024/11/2024-dora-report/), Nov 2024; the research could not check this
  against the primary report). In 2025, AI adoption "does continue to have a negative relationship with
  software delivery stability". DORA's summary: "AI doesn't fix a team; it amplifies what's already there"
  ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-the-2025-dora-report)).

The METR gap between felt and measured speed matters for you. Self-reported speedups are the weakest evidence
there is, and that includes your own.

### Review is now the bottleneck

- **Faros AI (2025):** teams with high AI adoption merged 98% more pull requests, but **PR review time rose
  91%**, PR size rose 154%, and bugs per developer rose 9%
  ([Augment Code summary](https://www.augmentcode.com/guides/ai-productivity-paradox-engineering-delivery)).
  Faros's own page says code changes doubled while company-level metrics stayed flat
  ([Faros AI](https://www.faros.ai/ai-productivity-paradox)).
- **Anthropic's study** found the same pressure from inside: one engineer said their work shifted "70%+"
  toward reviewing and revising AI code. More than half could fully delegate only 0–20% of their work. The
  study calls it a "paradox of supervision": overseeing AI needs the coding skills heavy AI use may erode
  ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/how-ai-is-transforming-work-at-anthropic)).
- **Stack Overflow's 2025 survey:** 46% distrust AI accuracy, 66% cite answers that are "almost right, but not
  quite", and 45.2% say debugging AI code takes longer ([Stack Overflow](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai)).

### Security, quality and incidents

- **Veracode** (2025-07-30) tested 80 tasks across more than 100 models. Generated code had a vulnerability in
  45% of cases, Java failed over 70% of the time, and models failed to defend against cross-site scripting in
  86% of cases and log injection in 88%. Larger models were not significantly better
  ([Veracode](https://www.veracode.com/press-release/ai-generated-code-poses-major-security-risks-in-nearly-half-of-all-development-tasks-veracode-research-reveals/)).
- **GitClear** (2025), across 211M changed lines: copy-pasted code rose from 8.3% (2021) to 12.3% (2024), and
  moved or refactored code fell from 25% to under 10%
  ([GitClear](https://www.gitclear.com/ai_assistant_code_quality_2025_research)).
- **Replit** (Jul 2025): its agent wiped a production database during a code freeze, then misreported what
  happened. Replit added automatic dev/prod database separation and a planning-only mode
  ([TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/searchsoftwarequality/news/366627829/Replit-AI-agent-snafu-shot-across-the-bow-for-vibe-coding)).
- **Amazon** (Mar 2026, per the FT): an internal note cited a "trend of incidents" with "high blast radius"
  and "Gen-AI assisted changes". Junior and mid-level engineers now need senior sign-off on AI-assisted
  changes. Amazon disputes parts of the reporting ([Slashdot relaying the FT](https://slashdot.org/story/26/03/10/212214/)).
  Note the response: **more senior review, not fewer engineers.**

### Where the verifiers come from

- **Stanford's "Canaries" paper** (revised 2026-08-12): employment for 22- to 25-year-olds in AI-exposed jobs is
  about 19% below its counterfactual, mainly from fewer hires. The authors find no economy-wide job losses and
  call the results descriptive, not causal
  ([Stanford Digital Economy Lab](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/canaries-in-the-coal-mine-six-facts-about-the-recent-employment-effects-of-artificial-intelligence/)).
- **Anthropic's skills trial** (2026-01-29): 52 mostly junior engineers learned a new library. The AI group
  scored 17 points lower on the follow-up quiz (50% vs 67%), with the biggest gap on debugging, and was not
  significantly faster. Those who asked conceptual questions kept their learning; those who fully delegated
  did not ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills)).

### Specialists, margins and layoffs

- **Specialist work:** on KernelBench, frontier models matched the PyTorch baseline in fewer than 20% of cases
  ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10517), Feb 2025). This is dated, and current models are likely better,
  but the research found no newer verified figure.
- **Revenue per employee hides inference bills.** AI coding startups have reported thin or negative gross
  margins because of model costs ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/07/the-high-costs-and-thin-margins-threatening-ai-coding-startups/), 2025-08-07).
  Part of the "small team" effect is spending moved from salaries to model providers.
- **"AI layoffs" are contested.** On Block's cut of about 4,000 jobs, a Mizuho analyst said "the vast majority
  of these cuts were probably not due to AI" ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/02/27/block-jack-dorsey-ceo-xyz-stock-square-4000-ai-layoffs/), 2026-02-27).

## Verdict: verification is the scarce skill

This is the guide's reading of the evidence, not a sourced finding.

The narrow form holds. Employers expect engineers to cover more of the lifecycle by directing agents, and
demand is moving toward senior roles. But the controlled evidence on raw speed is weak, stability gets worse
on average, review time grows, and the organizations that saw incidents responded by adding senior review.
What limits an engineer directing agents is not how much code they can generate. It's how much they can
**check**, and how well they choose what not to delegate.

That gives three practical consequences:

1. **AI-assisted rounds grade verification.** You're judged on whether you stayed in control and caught the
   model's mistakes, not on how clever your prompts were.
2. **No-AI rounds keep gating.** Fundamentals are what make verification possible. The supervision paradox is
   why companies still test you without tools.
3. **Specialists still matter where checking is expensive:** kernels, inference correctness, distributed
   systems, security and regulated systems. For AI Infra candidates, that is good news.

## What "good at working with agents" means

Practitioners and companies describe the same handful of skills. None of them is "writes great prompts".

### Decomposition

Break work into small tasks that can each be checked on their own. GitHub's spec-driven workflow goes
Specify, Plan, Tasks, Implement, so you review small, focused changes instead of large code dumps
([GitHub](https://github.blog/ai-and-ml/generative-ai/spec-driven-development-with-ai-get-started-with-a-new-open-source-toolkit/), 2025-09-02).
In an interview, this is the step candidates skip. State the plan before you prompt.

### Specs and context

Give the agent what it needs and nothing else. Anthropic's context-engineering guidance recommends system
prompts at the right level of detail, a small set of non-overlapping tools, a few canonical examples,
compaction, structured notes, sub-agents and just-in-time retrieval
([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents), 2025-09-29).

### Verification

Simon Willison's list of what makes agent work reliable reads like a senior engineer's checklist: automated
tests, planning up front, documentation, frequent commits, CI and linting, fast code review, manual QA and
edge-case probing, and judgment about what to delegate
([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Oct/7/vibe-engineering/), 2025-10-07). Addy Osmani's "70%
problem" makes the same point: treat AI output as a draft, then do the last 30% yourself, which is edge cases,
types and refactoring ([Addy Osmani](https://addyo.substack.com/p/the-70-problem-hard-truths-about), 2024-12-04).

### Evals as guardrails

For agents and LLM features, tests become evals. Anthropic's agent-eval guidance: start with 20–50 tasks drawn
from real failures, grade outcomes rather than steps, read transcripts, and promote capability evals into the
regression suite ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents), 2026-01-09).
It also distinguishes two numbers that are easy to confuse.

Saying which of the two your product needs is a quick, concrete Staff-level signal in an eval discussion.

### Cost and safety controls

Budgets, permissions and blast radius. The clearest evidence is incident-driven: Replit separated dev and prod
databases and added a plan-only mode, and Amazon added senior sign-off. The research found no standard
published rubric for this. In practice it means token and spend limits per run, least-privilege tool
permissions, human approval before destructive actions, and a way to roll back.

<AINative title="What this changes in a design round">
When you design an agent or an LLM feature, interviewers increasingly ask where the checks are. A strong answer
says which steps are deterministic code and which are model calls, where a human approves, what the eval gate
blocks on, and what the blast radius is if the model is confidently wrong. One Apple candidate was asked when
deterministic code beats an agent (<Cite id="s-d3df675c97" />, snippet). Treat "where does verification happen?"
as a required part of every AI design.
</AINative>

## How interviews grade it today

### AI-assisted rounds

- **Meta** (from about Oct 2025): 60 minutes in a multi-file project, in three phases (bug fix, core feature,
  optimize against tiered tests), with an assistant that answers in chat only. Hello Interview reports it is
  graded on problem solving, code quality, verification and communication, not prompting
  (<Cite id="s-810bf5f1ff" />). interviewing.io says Meta's materials call AI use "optional" with "no bearing
  on the outcome", and disputes that in practice (<Cite id="s-3b648144ca" />).
- **Canva** (official, Jun 2025): ambiguous, product-shaped problems such as an aircraft takeoff and landing
  control system. It rewards breaking down requirements, asking clarifying questions, finding and fixing
  problems in AI output, and production quality (<Cite id="s-88d61cb9fa" />).
- **Shopify:** an empty repo in your own IDE, any tool. Hello Interview says candidates who wrote no code by
  hand still passed when they directed the AI and could explain its output (<Cite id="s-5e3f4c7957" />).
- **LinkedIn:** CoderPad with an assistant that can't edit your code directly (<Cite id="s-4225f96d88" />). An
  Aug 2026 candidate went LRU, then LRU with TTL, then thread-safe, finished 2 of 4 tasks, and says most of the
  time went to fixing the AI's mistakes (<Cite id="s-8e19cb9489" />).
- **Snowflake:** an in-person rate-limiter round where the AI may write the code, but you show how you
  instructed, reviewed and tested it (<Cite id="s-5b59630431" />).
- **Microsoft CoreAI:** a candidate was told one round would use an IDE connected to an AI coding assistant
  (<Cite id="s-9766c0f8b8" />). A commenter described "a slightly higher bar since you have help" and advised
  "Review every suggestion before accepting" (<Cite id="s-02f49004af" />). A Microsoft SDE-2 design round required AI and graded prompting and judging the
  AI's answers (<Cite id="s-0b4f53a555" />).
- **Google's pilot** grades "AI fluency, including prompt engineering, output validation, and debugging
  skills", but only for junior and mid-level roles (<Cite id="s-90bac7273b" />, <Cite id="s-e5567e3f2c" />).

### What gets rewarded and what gets penalized

| Rewarded | Penalized |
|---|---|
| Stating your approach before the first prompt | Prompting for the whole solution |
| Running tests after every accepted change | Pasting code you can't explain |
| Catching invented helpers and wrong edge cases | Accepting confident wrong suggestions |
| Writing the core algorithm yourself when the AI struggles | Letting the agent drive the design |
| Asking the AI for review, tests or boilerplate | Doing everything by hand when AI is expected |

The rows come from Hello Interview's Meta guide (<Cite id="s-810bf5f1ff" />), Canva's post
(<Cite id="s-88d61cb9fa" />) and first-hand reports. A Senior Meta MLE candidate found the model inventing
helper functions that didn't exist (<Cite id="s-15a97c49b3" />). A Meta infra interviewer wanted the candidate
to write the code and use AI as a reviewer for readability and missing tests (<Cite id="s-27f34bf219" />).

<Pitfall title="Miscalibrating in either direction">
Too little AI is also marked down. A Meta MLE candidate's recruiter feedback was "less manual work, more on AI"
(<Cite id="s-443984bc07" />, Jul 2026). A rejected Stripe Staff candidate believes they let the agent drive when the
interviewer wanted decomposition and modular code (<Cite id="s-8e0784406e" />, Aug 2026). Ask the recruiter what
the round expects, then use the AI for the parts you can check quickly.
</Pitfall>

### Where AI is still banned or restricted

- **Anthropic:** "This is all you–no AI assistance unless we indicate otherwise" in live interviews, and no
  Claude on take-homes by default (<Cite id="s-b4d190c38f" />, last updated 2025-07-10, rechecked 2026-10-11).
  The performance take-home is the documented exception (<Cite id="s-a8c29f7dce" />).
- **OpenAI:** reportedly banned except in a beta agentic-coding round on a large codebase that not every
  candidate gets (<Cite id="s-74b419b5c0" />). A 2026 Forward Deployed Engineer candidate also reports an
  AI-enabled live coding screen (<Cite id="s-05342b6eb9" />). OpenAI's own guide could not be read.
- **Cursor:** first technical screens allow no AI beyond autocomplete (<Cite id="s-e3eeb47f1f" />).
- **Amazon:** no generative AI tools unless explicitly permitted (<Cite id="s-102d0e0e4f" />, Feb 2025), though its
  redesigned SDE assessment now includes an interactive AI assistant (<Cite id="s-6aabb0c00d" />, undated).
- **Staff+ infra loops:** a Staff+ SRE who interviewed at AI-infra companies in early 2026 says every loop
  expected no AI use during the interview, and he was surprised "how rarely anyone asked how I use or would use
  LLMs"
  ([Dave Beckett](https://www.dajobe.org/blog/2026/04/20/llm-gap-in-staff-plus-interviews/), 2026-04-20).

So prepare for both. The full per-company list is on the
[companies page](/system-design-labs/interviews/companies/).

## Practice drills

Each drill takes an hour or less. Do each one at least twice, a week apart.

### 1. AI-assisted round (60 minutes)

Use a lab repo you haven't read as the unfamiliar codebase. The Go
<LabRef lab="kafka-lite" inline /> or <LabRef lab="rate-limiter" inline /> labs work well: their tests passed when last
run (one rate-limiter test is flaky), so a failure you see is most likely one you caused.

1. **Bug fix (15 min), by hand.** Have a friend, or an agent, plant a bug without telling you where. Find and
   fix it without AI.
2. **Feature (30 min), with AI.** Add something real, such as a retention limit per topic or a per-tenant
   limit. Say your plan out loud before the first prompt. Run tests after every accepted change.
3. **Optimize (15 min).** Write a larger test input and make it pass within a time limit.

Afterwards, score yourself on Meta's four axes: problem solving, code quality, verification, communication.
Then explain every AI-written function line by line to someone, or to a recording.

### 2. Review an AI-generated PR (45 minutes)

Have an agent write a 300- to 500-line change you didn't plan in detail. Review it as if a junior engineer sent
it. Look for the failure classes Veracode measured (input injection, cross-site scripting, log injection),
plus auth gaps, races and swallowed errors. Write down which parts you'd ask to split into smaller PRs and the
blast radius of each bug you find. A reported Jun 2026 Microsoft question title asks candidates to find the bugs in an AI-generated URL
shortener (<Cite id="s-d302bba651" />), so this isn't only an exercise.

### 3. Spec and decomposition (30 minutes, no code)

Take Canva's example problem: an aircraft takeoff and landing control system. Write a one-page spec,
a plan, and a task list where each task has acceptance tests. Stop before writing any code. Then check: could an
agent do each task, and could you verify each result in under five minutes?

### 4. Eval design (45 minutes)

Pick a RAG feature or an agent. Design:

- 20–50 tasks built from failures you've seen or can imagine,
- a grader for each (exact match, code check, model-based judge, or human),
- whether you report pass@k or pass^k, and why,
- how the eval gates a prompt or model change in CI, and what it blocks on.

Then build the smallest version with the <LabRef lab="rag" inline /> and <LabRef lab="llm-eval" inline /> labs.

### 5. No-AI fundamentals (ongoing)

Keep one timed algorithm or concurrency problem a week, by hand. The banned-AI rounds above aren't going
away soon, and fundamentals are what make the other four drills possible.

## Talking about AI-driven impact in behavioral rounds

Expect questions like "How do you use AI tools?" or "Tell me about bad AI output you caught". Both appear in
reported 2026 question titles (<Cite id="s-d302bba651" />, <Cite id="s-56187f9727" />). Meta's 2026 review
criteria for employees give a useful frame: your own productivity, and tools you built that lifted the team.

How to answer without overclaiming:

- **Lead with an outcome, not usage.** "Moved N services to the new client in W weeks, with zero rollbacks"
  beats "I use Claude Code every day". Fill in your own measured numbers.
- **Say what you verified and how.** Tests you wrote first, the review step, the eval that gated the change.
- **Include a failure you caught.** A wrong migration, an invented API, a security hole in generated code.
  This is the story interviewers remember.
- **Name what you didn't delegate,** and why: the design, the data model, the rollout plan.
- **Be honest about measurement.** If your speedup is self-reported, say so. METR's developers believed they
  were about 20% faster while measuring 19% slower.

<Pitfall title="'AI writes 80% of my code' is not an impact claim">
It describes usage, and interviewers can't check it. A UK head of engineering told the Pragmatic Engineer in 2026
that résumés from candidates with mid-level skills now list AI keywords ("RAG, evals, inference…") that don't
hold up in interviews. A startup tech lead said: "I'd rather hire someone who is 'behind' on AI, but has great
taste/judgment"
([Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/tech-jobs-market-in-2026-part-3-hiring), 2026-07-07).
Replace the percentage with what shipped, what it changed, and what you checked.
</Pitfall>
